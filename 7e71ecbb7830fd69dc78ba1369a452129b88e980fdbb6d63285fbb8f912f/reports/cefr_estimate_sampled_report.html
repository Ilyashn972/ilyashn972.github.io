Are GPT summaries simpler — or just more written?

We compared three versions of the same 12 spoken stories: the original telling, people retelling from memory, and GPT-4o summaries (108 texts). The boxes below are averages, always in the same order: original story · human recall · GPT summary.

A note on “CEFR”. CEFR (A1–C2) is a school scale for English learners. These texts are native spoken stories, not exam scripts. A phrase like we was is ordinary talk, not a beginner mistake. That is why the first five boxes measure how spoken, packed, and specific the wording is. The last box is a CEFR-like score we still report, with that caveat.
The short answer. GPT summaries do not just look “simpler English.” They look more like writing: denser, more unusual word choices, and much shorter sentences. The original stories sound the most like speech.

What the numbers mean

Each box shows three averages: original story · human recall · GPT summary. You do not need the plots below to get the main result.

Spoken vs written (register)
A simple count of talk-like cues (pronouns, contractions, “and then”) minus write-like cues (long words, packing). It is not a quality score. A negative number just means “more written than spoken.”
How packed the words are (lexical density)
0.47 means about 47% of the words are content words. A recipe or a news summary is usually denser than a conversation.
Vocabulary variety (MATTR)
Close to 1 means almost every word is new; lower means more repetition. All three kinds sit around 0.75, so variety is not what separates them.
Unusual-word share (rare-word rate)
0.53 means about half the content words were off a common-word list. Summaries name events tightly (scoutmaster, cramps) instead of chatting around them.
Words per sentence
GPT summaries average about 8 words per sentence; recalls about 22. That is packing, not “the model cannot write a long sentence.”
CEFR-like score
1 ≈ A1, 3 ≈ B1, 4 ≈ B2, 6 ≈ C2. We asked the model to judge range of language, not whether the speaker would pass an exam. Standard-English “accuracy” is plotted separately later, so dialect does not drag the overall score down by itself.

How to read this

  • story: the original spoken narrative.
  • recall: a person retelling that story from memory (4 per story, short to long).
  • summary: a GPT-4o key-point summary (4 per story, from few points to many).
  • The CEFR-like scores are a best-effort estimate, not an official exam result.
  • We asked GPT to write very simple key points, so some “simplicity” is the instruction, not a natural speaking style.
