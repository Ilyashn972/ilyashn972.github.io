Narrative m-bits · human-recall brevity

What happens if we rewrite a recall more briefly without changing the events?

Human recalls lose to GPT summaries on m-bit rate. This experiment compresses the recall itself: same event set, shorter wording, then rescores.

On this page: Predictions Design Findings Plots Tables Example Limits

Predictions (written before looking at numbers)

Rate is m-bits ÷ context characters. Three nested bets:

  1. If the gate holds and only wording shrinks: length down, total m-bits roughly stable, rate up. That is the identity, not a memory claim.
  2. If the model still drops events: total m-bits fall with length (anatomy simple_brief on paste: rate 0.10→0.22 but m-bits 145→83). The segment-set gate is there to catch this; primary tables use verify_ok only.
  3. A clean rate gain need not close the GPT-summary gap. Costa’s recall-scale comparison already shows extractive summary > matched recall at the same length. A brief human recall tests density of human-selected events; leftover gap vs GPT summaries is still which events.

Design

Same seed-42 sample as the efficiency study: 4 length-quartile human recalls × 4 rephrased GPT summary targets per story. GPT-4o rewrites each recall with the prompt below. We map the rewrite back onto narrative segments and keep it only when it stays on the original recall’s event set Sr (Jaccard ≥ 0.8, coverage ≥ 0.85, extra-segment fraction ≤ 0.15).

Scorer: Llama-3.3 --- get_mbits. Primary target = held-out rephrased summaries. Secondary = rephrased full narrative. Original-recall summary-target cells reuse the efficiency-sample scores when the pair matches. 

Rewrite the recall below more briefly.

Rules:
- Do not add any facts, events, people, or details that are not in the original.
- Do not remove any facts, events, people, or details that are in the original.
- You may drop only filler, repetition, hedges, and wording that does not change meaning.
- Keep the same grammatical person and voice as the original (first person stays first person).

Return JSON only:
```json
{
  "text": "brief rewrite"
}
```

Recall:
Gate passed for 8 / 48 brief rewrites (48 sampled recalls). Failed rewrites are scored but dropped from the primary means.

What we found

Matched pairs only (the 8 recalls whose rewrite passed the event-set gate). Original mean rate 0.1458 vs brief 0.2003 (Δ +0.0545). Mean m-bits 55.7 → 54.7 (Δ -1.0). Mean length 460 → 302 (Δ -158). Rate rose for 8 / 8 paired recalls. Total m-bits stayed within 15% for 6 / 8. The gate is strict: most rewrites look like they dropped events to the mapper (prediction 2), so the primary claim is only about the 8 that kept Sr.

schissel_v1-pool · 5e25ca0e637b4103e19e7168 Δ rate +0.0853 · length 189 → 125

Original

The person was at their cousins wedding and got pushed into the pool. The friend spotted a bee by their mouth so tried to push the person away from it but pushed them into the pool instead.

Brief rewrite

At my cousin's wedding, a friend saw a bee near my mouth and tried to push me away, but accidentally pushed me into the pool.

Limits

  • The gate uses an LLM clause→segment map, not human annotation of the rewrite.
  • Same-event is not the same as same total m-bits: register can still move rate on frozen S (see fixed-S controls).
  • Sampled design (4 recalls × 4 summary targets), not every recall.
  • Anatomy simple_brief compressed extractive paste, not human recall, and did not forbid dropping facts.
