Do m-bits and p-caught mark the same segments?

Chapter 3 showed that human recalls and GPT-4o summaries share a segment m-bits profile (mean Pearson r = 0.878). That is agreement within m-bits. This page asks whether those high-m-bits positions are simply the high-p-caught positions.

Question

p-caught is a mention rate: the fraction of recalls (or GPT summaries) that map onto a narrative segment at least once. m-bits is predictive mass: mean per-segment logp from those texts against the original narrative. A beat can be mentioned often and still add almost no m-bits (short, repeated, or already predictable), or carry high m-bits despite being mentioned less often.

Answer (12-story mean, equal story weight). On average, no. Mean rank correlation is only moderate (0.41); the hottest p-caught beats are usually not the hottest m-bits beats. One story exceeds ρ = 0.7 (Triplett v2 Cat Lady).
ρ recall m-bits vs p-caught Mean Spearman 0.406. 1/12 stories above 0.7; 9/12 below 0.5.
ρ summary m-bits vs p-caught Mean Spearman 0.397. GPT predictive mass vs human mention rates.
Top-quartile Jaccard Mean 0.396 (recall) / 0.377 (summary). Hottest 25% sets only partly overlap.
ρ summary vs GPT p-caught Mean Spearman 0.396. Secondary check: GPT mentions vs GPT m-bits.

Totals average the 12 story-level statistics (Costa’s 194 segments do not outweigh Boy Scout’s 19). Top-k Jaccard at k=3, 5, 7 is a larger fraction of short stories; top-quartile Jaccard is the length-fair overlap number. Ties broken by segment_id.

All stories together

Spearman ρ asks whether the two measures put segments in the same order. Chapter 3’s recall-vs-summary m-bits Pearson r (mean 0.878) is a different comparison: two m-bits profiles, not m-bits vs p-caught.
Jaccard of the hottest 25% asks a stricter question: are the same sticky beats in the highlight reel? Rank correlation can be high while the top sets still differ.
