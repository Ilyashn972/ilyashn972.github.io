Narrative m-bits · recall-scale held-out context comparison

Which context predicts other Costa summaries?

At lengths typical of a human Costa recall, we hold out one GPT-4o summary and ask how well that text — versus a matched recall, an extractive condensation, or a random excerpt — predicts the remaining summaries.

On this page: Question Design Not LOO Results How to read Contexts Next question Legacy files

The question

Costa pairwise rates already show GPT-4o summaries beating human recalls when predicting other GPT-4o summaries. That comparison mixes many lengths. Here we freeze length near a typical recall (Costa mean 1071 characters, median 996; n=93) and ask: does the summary still win when every context is recall-sized?

The four preceding contexts are length-matched. The targets are the other GPT-4o summaries. We emphasize rephrased targets so shared GPT-4o wording is not the whole story.

What we actually do

  1. Pick the GPT-4o summary whose character length is closest to a target of 1000 or 1175.
  2. Hold that summary out as a context (not a training fold).
  3. Score total m-bits of every remaining summary against four preceding texts of similar length:
    • Held-out summary — the GPT-4o summary itself.
    • Extractive summary — an LLM-prompted condensation that keeps selected narrative segments.
    • Matched recall — the human recall closest in character count.
    • Random excerpt — randomly chosen narrative segments concatenated to a similar length.
  4. Repeat with original vs rephrased wording of contexts and targets.

Realized context lengths at tag 1000: held-out summary 975, recall 998, extractive 1188, random excerpt 1057. At tag 1175: 1173 / 1175 / 1188 / 1057.

Why this is not “leave-one-out”

Older pages called this a leave-one-out analysis. That name is misleading in two ways, so this hub no longer uses it.

  • It is not ML-style LOO (train on n−1, test on one). We hold out one summary as a preceding context and score how well it predicts the others.
  • It is not “drop Costa from the 12-story average” of narrative-target correlations. That sensitivity check answers a different question on a different target.

What we found

Same ordering at both recall-scale lengths, on rephrased targets: held-out summary > extractive summary > matched recall > random excerpt. At ~1000 characters the means are 203 > 170 > 109 > 48 m-bits. At ~1175 they are 237 > 170 > 132 > 48. Length alone, and arbitrary sampling of the narrative, do not explain the summary-context advantage.
Held-out summary 1000: 202.6 · 1175: 237.2 m-bits
Extractive summary both tags: 169.8 m-bits (84% / 72% of held-out)
Matched recall 1000: 109.4 (54%) · 1175: 132.1 (56%)
Random excerpt both tags: 47.9 m-bits

How to read the interactive plots

Each linked plot is a scatter of total m-bits (y) against summary length (x) for the remaining GPT-4o summaries. Circles are original wording; crosses are GPT-4o rephrases. Horizontal spread is the length of the target summaries, not of the four contexts — those contexts are already length-matched.

Unlike the 12-story narrative figures, this comparison is about amount of predictive information at a fixed context length, so the vertical axis is total m-bits rather than rate. Rate is still in the tables (m-bits ÷ context characters).

Open next question: is recall just verbose?

Matched recall loses on rate as well as total m-bits (0.061 vs 0.116 at tag 1000). That could be extra words around the same events, or a different event set. The direct test on these matched Costa recalls is a meaning-preserving brevity rewrite, plotted as rate against every remaining rephrased summary: length 1000 · length 1175. The 12-story sample of other recalls is recall_brevity_rewrite_report.html.
